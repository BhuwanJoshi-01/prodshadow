# OBS001 · lock-wait-budget

| Default severity | Evidence |
|---|---|
| critical | observed |

## What it detects

A workload session waited longer than `policy.max_lock_wait_ms` (default 2000 ms) while the migration ran. The observed value is the largest of three measurements: the longest sampled lock wait (`pg_locks.waitstart`), the longest wait recorded in the server log (`log_lock_waits`), and the longest client-side stall — an operation that started during the migration and took longer than the scenario's baseline p99.

## Why it matters

This is the user-visible symptom of a blocking migration: requests hang. The budget states how long your application can tolerate a stall before timeouts, retries and connection-pool exhaustion turn it into an outage.

## Example

Flagged:

```yaml
policy:
  max_lock_wait_ms: 2000
```

Preferred:

```yaml
policy:
  # match your application's request timeout, minus a margin
  max_lock_wait_ms: 800
```

## How ProdShadow verifies it

Confidence is high when every one of at least three candidate repeats exceeded the budget, and lowered when only client-side evidence exists. The finding points at the migration statement most likely responsible (the longest-running statement that takes a blocking lock).

## Configuration

Change the severity or turn the rule off in `prodshadow.yaml`:

```yaml
policy:
  rules:
    OBS001: critical   # or info, warning, off
```

Suppress a reviewed case next to the statement:

```sql
-- prodshadow:ignore OBS001 reason="why this is acceptable" expires=2027-06-30
```
